PATIENT CLINICAL NOTE
=====================
Date: 2024-02-28
Patient ID: PT-005
Age: 22 | Gender: Male

CHIEF COMPLAINT:
Sudden onset severe headache, high fever, and stiff neck.
Brought to ER by roommate due to altered consciousness.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
Patient's roommate reports the patient complained of the "worst headache of
his life" approximately 6 hours ago. Since then, he has developed a high
fever reaching 40.1°C, stiff neck (nuchal rigidity), and extreme sensitivity
to light and sound. Over the past 2 hours, he has become increasingly
confused and difficult to rouse. No recent travel history but attended a
crowded indoor event 5 days ago.

SYMPTOMS REPORTED:
- Severe headache (sudden onset, "thunderclap")
- High fever (40.1°C / 104.2°F)
- Stiff neck (nuchal rigidity — confirmed on exam)
- Sensitivity to light (photophobia)
- Sensitivity to sound (phonophobia)
- Altered consciousness / confusion
- Nausea and vomiting
- Rash (non-blanching petechial rash on trunk — noted on exam)
- Chills

CURRENT MEDICATIONS:
None.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
No significant past medical history. Vaccinated as a child.
Meningococcal vaccine status unknown.

NEUROLOGICAL EXAM:
- GCS: 12/15 (E3V4M5)
- Kernig's sign: Positive
- Brudzinski's sign: Positive
- Pupils: Equal and reactive, 4mm bilaterally
- Neck stiffness: Severe — chin-to-chest impossible

INVESTIGATIONS:
- CT Head: No contraindication to lumbar puncture
- Lumbar Puncture ordered — pending
- WBC: 18,500 (markedly elevated)
- CRP: 210 mg/L (critically elevated)

VITALS:
Temp: 40.1°C | HR: 118 bpm | BP: 98/64 mmHg | SpO2: 96%

ASSESSMENT:
Bacterial meningitis — high clinical suspicion.
Classic triad present: fever + neck rigidity + altered consciousness.
Non-blanching rash raises concern for meningococcal septicemia.

PLAN:
- EMERGENCY: IV ceftriaxone 2g initiated immediately (do not delay for LP)
- IV dexamethasone 0.15mg/kg to reduce inflammation
- Blood cultures before antibiotics if possible
- Isolation precautions — droplet
- Neurology and Infectious Disease consult
- ICU transfer
- Notify public health for contact tracing
