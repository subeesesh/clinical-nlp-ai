PATIENT CLINICAL NOTE
=====================
Date: 2024-08-22
Patient ID: PT-009
Age: 31 | Gender: Male

CHIEF COMPLAINT:
Severe diarrhea, vomiting, and abdominal cramping for the past 36 hours
following a work lunch event.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
Patient attended a work catered lunch 2 days ago. Approximately 8 hours
afterward, he developed sudden onset nausea followed by vomiting (6 episodes).
He has had watery diarrhea up to 10 times in the past 24 hours with severe
abdominal cramps. He reports a mild fever of 38.2°C and has been unable to
keep fluids down. He notes three colleagues who also attended the event have
similar symptoms. He is showing signs of mild dehydration.

SYMPTOMS REPORTED:
- Diarrhea (watery, 8–10 episodes per day)
- Nausea and vomiting (6 episodes in past 24 hours)
- Severe abdominal pain and stomach cramps
- Loss of appetite (complete)
- Low-grade fever (38.2°C)
- Fatigue and weakness
- Dizziness on standing (orthostatic)
- Dry mouth and reduced urine output (dehydration signs)
- Mild headache

CURRENT MEDICATIONS:
None. Attempted oral rehydration at home — unable to tolerate.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
No chronic GI conditions. No recent antibiotics. No IBD.

EPIDEMIOLOGY:
Likely food poisoning — cluster of cases at same catered event.
Possible pathogens: Salmonella, Campylobacter, or Staphylococcal toxin.

INVESTIGATIONS:
- Stool culture: Ordered (pending)
- Blood tests: WBC 11,800, CRP 28 mg/L (mildly elevated)
- Electrolytes: Na+ 132 (mildly low), K+ 3.1 (low — due to losses)
- Urine: Dark, concentrated — oliguria present

VITALS:
Temp: 38.3°C | HR: 108 bpm | BP: 102/68 mmHg | SpO2: 98%
Weight: Down 2.5 kg from baseline (fluid loss)

ASSESSMENT:
Acute viral/bacterial gastroenteritis — likely foodborne illness.
Mild-moderate dehydration with electrolyte imbalance.

PLAN:
- IV fluid resuscitation: Normal saline 1L bolus, then maintenance
- IV potassium replacement
- Antiemetic: Ondansetron 4mg IV
- Oral rehydration solution once vomiting controlled
- Stool culture result awaited — antibiotics on hold unless culture positive
- Bland BRAT diet when tolerating oral intake
- Discharge when oral intake stable and vitals normalised
- Advise to report symptoms to environmental health (possible outbreak)
